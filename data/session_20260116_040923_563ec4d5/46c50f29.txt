
Natural Language Processing enables computers to interact with human language in meaningful ways. It bridges the gap
between linguistic theory and machine computation. NLP systems analyze syntax, semantics, and context to derive meaning
from text and speech.

Tokenization, stemming, and lemmatization are foundational preprocessing steps. Language models learn probability
distributions over word sequences. Modern NLP relies heavily on deep learning architectures such as transformers.

Applications of NLP include chatbots, sentiment analysis, document summarization, and machine translation. These systems
are used in customer support, social media monitoring, and legal document analysis.

Challenges in NLP involve ambiguity, sarcasm, multilingual processing, and domain adaptation. Large datasets and
pretrained models have significantly improved performance but require substantial computational resources.

Ethical concerns include misinformation generation, bias in language models, and data privacy. Responsible deployment
requires careful evaluation and governance.

Future NLP research focuses on low‑resource languages, reasoning over long documents, and alignment with human values.
